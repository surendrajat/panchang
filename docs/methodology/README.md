# How a Hindu Calendar Works — from scratch

*The math, the code, and the **why** behind the पञ्चाङ्ग (pañcāṅga).*

This is a teaching course, not a reference manual. It starts from "what is the
Moon doing tonight" and builds, step by step, to a calendar that can tell you
the तिथि (lunar day), the festival dates, and the birth chart for **any place on
Earth and any moment from 1500 CE to 2500 CE** — and explains why each piece
exists and how we make it accurate. Every lesson points at the exact code in
this repository that implements it, so you can read the idea and then read the
20 lines that do it.

A note on the Sanskrit/Hindi words: they carry meaning that the English loses,
so this course keeps them — always with the Devanagari and a transliteration the
first time, e.g. तिथि (*tithi*). There is a [glossary](./glossary.md) at the end.

---

## 1. The big picture: why this calendar is *luni-solar*

Almost every calendar is a compromise between two clocks in the sky that
**refuse to divide evenly**:

- **The Moon** (चन्द्र, *candra*) is the easy clock. Anyone can see its phases;
  from one new moon to the next is about **29.53 days** — a natural "month."
- **The Sun** (सूर्य, *sūrya*) is the important clock. It rules the seasons and
  the harvest; one full cycle of seasons is about **365.24 days** — a "year."

The trouble: a year is **not** a whole number of lunar months (365.24 / 29.53 ≈
12.37). So you must choose what to sacrifice:

| Calendar | Follows | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Islamic (Hijri) | only the Moon | drifts through the seasons ~11 days/year |
| Gregorian | only the Sun | months have nothing to do with the Moon |
| **Hindu** | **both** | needs a leap *month* every ~3 years to stay aligned |

The Hindu calendar refuses to sacrifice either. It counts months by the Moon
**and** keeps them locked to the Sun's seasons by occasionally inserting a
**leap month** — the अधिक मास (*adhika māsa*), which you'll meet in
[Lesson 9](./09-masa.md). That single decision — *keep both clocks* — is the
source of everything that follows, including all the beauty and all the
complications.

## 2. The one idea that makes the whole thing tractable

Here is the secret that turns "a 2000-year-old astronomical tradition" into
"a few hundred lines of code":

> **Everything reduces to two numbers** — the position of the Sun and the
> position of the Moon along the zodiac, at the instant you care about.

"Position along the zodiac" means the **ecliptic longitude**: an angle from
0° to 360° measured along the Sun's yearly path. Call them λ☉ (Sun) and
λ☽ (Moon). Then, astonishingly, *all five limbs of the pañcāṅga are just
arithmetic on these two angles*:

| Limb | Hindi | What it is | Formula (sketch) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tithi** | तिथि | lunar day | from **λ☽ − λ☉** (Moon's lead over Sun) |
| **Nakshatra** | नक्षत्र | lunar mansion | from **λ☽** alone |
| **Yoga** | योग | a Sun+Moon angle | from **λ☽ + λ☉** |
| **Karana** | करण | half-tithi | from **λ☽ − λ☉** |
| **Vara** | वार | weekday | from the civil day (the one piece that isn't λ) |

So the course has a natural shape. **First** we learn to compute λ☉ and λ☽
accurately for any time and place (Lessons 1–5). **Then** everything else is
definitions and bookkeeping on top (Lessons 6–12).

## 3. The accuracy philosophy (what makes *this* implementation trustworthy)

Old pañcāṅgas computed λ☉ and λ☽ with the *Sūrya Siddhānta* — clever
medieval formulas that drift by up to a degree today. Modern ones use **dṛk
gaṇita** (दृक् गणित, "observation-based mathematics"): real ephemeris models
fit to centuries of telescope and spacecraft data. This project is dṛk gaṇita.
Three rules keep it honest, and you'll see them enforced in every lesson:

1. **Compute from first principles, not from a lookup of someone else's
   answer.** The Sun and Moon come from [`astronomy-engine`][ae] (a port of
   NASA-grade models); the calendar logic is the classical definitions applied
   to those positions.
2. **Validate against an independent gold standard.** We check our output
   against the **Swiss Ephemeris** (the library `astro.com` and professional
   software use) — *not* against the popular website we might be tempted to
   copy. Those checks live in `tests/regression/*-vs-swisseph.test.ts` and are
   regenerated by a committed script, [`tests/reference/gen_swisseph.py`](../../tests/reference/).
3. **Separate facts from conventions, and say which is which.** That the Moon
   is at a certain longitude is a *fact*. Which day a festival is observed when
   a tithi is skipped is a *convention* — and traditions genuinely disagree.
   Where we follow a convention (often the popular Drik Panchang), we label it;
   where we can't cite a source, we say so. [Lesson 11](./11-festivals.md) is
   blunt about this.

[ae]: https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy

## 4. The course

Read in order — each lesson assumes the previous ones. Every lesson ends with
the files in `src/` that implement it.

**Part I — Foundations (telling *when* and *where*)**
- **[1 · Keeping time](./01-time.md)** — civil time, time zones, and the
  **Julian Day** (the single number that names an instant). `astro/julian.ts`.
- **[2 · The sky](./02-the-sky.md)** — the ecliptic (क्रान्तिवृत्त), ecliptic
  longitude, and sidereal time. Where a "position in the zodiac" actually is.

**Part II — The two lights and the fixed zodiac**
- **[3 · Sūrya and Candra](./03-sun-and-moon.md)** — computing λ☉ and λ☽:
  apparent geocentric longitudes, and why "apparent" matters. `astro/ephemeris.ts`.
- **[4 · Precession and the अयनांश](./04-precession-and-ayanamsa.md)** — the
  *tropical* vs *sidereal* zodiac, the slow wobble of the equinox, and the
  ayanāṁśa correction. The honest story of "which Lahiri." `astro/ayanamsa.ts`.
- **[5 · नक्षत्र and राशि](./05-nakshatra-and-rashi.md)** — cutting the sidereal
  circle into 27 mansions and 12 signs.

**Part III — The five limbs (पञ्चाङ्ग)**
- **[6 · तिथि — the lunar day](./06-tithi.md)** — the heart of the calendar:
  the Sun–Moon elongation, why a tithi isn't 24 hours, and how we find the exact
  instant it ends. `panchanga/tithi.ts`. *(written — the worked example)*
- **[7 · Yoga, Karana, Vara](./07-yoga-karana-vara.md)** — the remaining limbs.

**Part IV — Building the calendar**
- **[8 · The day and सूर्योदय](./08-the-day.md)** — why the Hindu day begins at
  sunrise, and how we compute sunrise for any latitude. `astro/sunrise.ts`.
- **[9 · मास — the lunar month](./09-masa.md)** — amānta vs pūrṇimānta, naming a
  month by the Sun, and the leap (अधिक) and lost (क्षय) months. `panchanga/masa.ts`.
- **[10 · The year](./10-the-year.md)** — संवत् era counts, ऋतु seasons, the
  अयन (solstice halves), and संक्रान्ति (the Sun changing sign).

**Part V — Application**
- **[11 · Festivals](./11-festivals.md)** — how a tithi becomes a date on the
  wall: the *vyāpinī* rules, the tie-breakers, and an honest accounting of where
  we match tradition and where we can't.
- **[12 · कुण्डली — the birth chart](./12-jyotish.md)** — the same Sun/Moon
  machinery, turned on a moment of birth: लग्न, ग्रह, भाव, दशा.

- **[Glossary](./glossary.md)** — every Sanskrit/Hindi term, with Devanagari.

## 5. How to read the code alongside

The repository is organized exactly like this course:

```
src/lib/
  astro/       ← Lessons 1–4, 8 : time, ephemeris, ayanamsa, sunrise
  panchanga/   ← Lessons 5–11   : tithi, nakshatra, yoga, masa, festivals
  jyotish/     ← Lesson 12       : the kundli engine
```

When a lesson says *"see `panchanga/tithi.ts`"*, open it — the functions are
small and named after the concept (`tithiAtJD`, `nextTithiEnd`). The goal is
that after this course you could delete a file and rewrite it from the lesson.
